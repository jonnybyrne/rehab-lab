Why does a humid 75°F morning feel so bad?

Why does humidity, not the temperature, decide how a run feels?

The headline number
Dew point breakpoints: humid above 65°F, oppressive above 75°F

A humid 75°F morning can feel worse than a dry 90°F afternoon, because sweat cools you only as fast as the surrounding air accepts water.

On the runner’s dew-point chart the essay examines, above 65°F feels humid and above 75°F feels oppressive, breakpoints that line up broadly with meteorological guidance.

Ely and colleagues grouped decades of results from seven marathons by heat stress and found slower times as heat rose. The essay explains why the dew point, not the thermometer, is the number to watch.

Sources: Ely et al. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise. 2008. Che Muhamed et al. Temperature. 2016.
