Walking is controlled falling.

What happens, joint by joint, in one second of walking?

The headline number
Eight phases in one stride; the center of mass rides a path of about 5 cm

One second of walking divides into eight phases, and the essay lets you scrub the stride yourself: figure, clock, phase name, and joint readouts all run from the same normative teaching values.

The classic frame underneath is the six determinants paper of 1953, which put the body’s center of mass on a smooth path of about 5 centimeters.

The essay also airs the later critique: whether those determinants really flatten that path, or explain why walking is efficient, is still argued.

Sources: Saunders, Inman & Eberhart. Journal of Bone and Joint Surgery, American Volume. 1953.
