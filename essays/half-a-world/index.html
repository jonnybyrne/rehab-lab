What If Half Your World Disappeared?

Your eyes work, and breakfast is right in front of you. Why does the left half of the plate not exist?

The headline number
Neglect appeared in 43 percent of right hemisphere strokes and 20 percent of left in an acute cohort of 750 people (Ringman et al. 2004)

After a right hemisphere stroke, the left side of the world can stop registering: not because the eyes fail, but because attention does. Food stays on the left of the plate, clock numbers crowd to the right, and the wheelchair clips every left doorframe.

The essay walks one room as a camera, from the whole room to the half room and back: the plate, the clock, the line bisection and star cancellation tests, the reading page, and the corridor, then the scanning anchors and prism adaptation that teach the left side back.

The evidence is honest in both directions. Scanning training carries the strongest guideline grade; pooled cognitive rehabilitation improved test scores without a persisting effect on everyday activities, and prism adaptation showed no benefit in a later sham controlled meta analysis.

Sources: Ringman JM, et al. Neurology. 2004. Azouvi P, et al. 2002; 2003. Rossetti Y, et al. Nature. 1998. Cicerone KD, et al. 2019. Bowen A, et al. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. 2013.
