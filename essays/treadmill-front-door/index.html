The Treadmill and the Front Door

The most advanced walking machine in stroke rehab was matched stride for stride by home exercises that barely practised walking. How?

The headline number
52.0 percent of all 408 participants improved functional walking at one year; early locomotor training versus home exercise adjusted odds ratio 0.83, late locomotor training versus home exercise 1.19 (Duncan et al., New England Journal of Medicine 2011)

LEAPS randomised 408 people, two months after stroke, to early locomotor training with a harness over a treadmill, the same training started late, or a structured home exercise programme run by a physical therapist.

At one year, just over half of everyone in the trial had improved their functional walking. Neither locomotor group beat the home programme: both confidence intervals include no difference, and the essay says so plainly.

There was no untreated arm, so the shared improvement cannot be credited to either programme; it may be recovery, attention, or practice of any kind. The trial rules out superiority of the machine, not the value of walking practice.

Sources: Duncan PW, et al. N Engl J Med. 2011;364:2026 to 2036.
