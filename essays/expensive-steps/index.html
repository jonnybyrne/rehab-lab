The Most Expensive Steps

Why do some steps cost so much more oxygen than others?

The headline number
Walking after stroke: 1.5 to 2 times the oxygen cost of typical gait

Two ways to bill a walk. Only one is the fare. There are two ways to bill a walk. O₂ rate (milliliters per kilo per minute) is how hard the engine works. O₂ cost (milliliters per kilo per meter) is the actual fare. People almost always slow down to protect the engine, so cost per meter is the comparison that counts. Translated into calories at about 5 kcal per liter of oxygen, for a 70 kg walker: about 560 calories for the first day, about 290 for the second.

Transfemoral walkers average 1,540 steps a day. In these two modeled receipts, the walker paying the most per step takes the fewest steps, and still ends the day ahead on oxygen. That is a comparison of the two receipts above, not a ranking of every walker.

Five active transtibial men walked and ran in three feet. The energy-storing Flex-Foot beat the SACH nowhere: no significant walking difference.

Thirteen transtibial walkers, five feet of differing forefoot flexibility. No differences in oxygen cost. Subjects simply preferred the most ankle-like.

Doubly-labeled water, the gold standard for total daily energy, found microprocessor knees changed no oxygen per unit distance. Users just walked more.

Sources: Macko et al. Stroke. 1997. Waters & Mulroy. Gait & Posture. 1999.
