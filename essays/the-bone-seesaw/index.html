The Bone Seesaw

Why does bone vanish below a spinal cord injury, and why does it break during an ordinary transfer?

The headline number
Proximal tibia density 70 percent below controls; fractures at 2.2 percent per year

Two crews keep bone strong: one builds new bone, one removes old bone. Below a spinal cord injury, that balance breaks: removal speeds up, and building does not keep up.

In 31 men studied more than a year after injury, bone mineral density at the proximal tibia sat 70 percent below age-matched controls, and at the distal femur 52 percent below.

Then the breaks arrive during ordinary transfers. Across the long follow-up the essay traces, fracture incidence ran at 2.2 percent per year. The essay follows the seesaw, the scan that can lie about it, and the drugs that push back.

Sources: Dauty M, et al. Bone. 2000. Zehnder Y, et al. 2004. Dudley-Javoroski S, Shields RK. 2012.
