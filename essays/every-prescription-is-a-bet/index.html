Every Prescription Is a Bet

When is a prescription a good bet, and for whom?

The headline number
ASCEND: one event prevented per 91 treated; one major bleed per 111 treated

Medicine runs on odds. This essay reads one prescription the way an actuary would: as a priced bet on a single life.

Its opening slip prices aspirin in the ASCEND trial: one serious vascular event prevented for every 91 people treated over 7.4 years, and one major bleed caused for every 111.

The same drug is a great bet for one patient and a poor one for another. Nothing about the drug changes; only the odds do, and the essay shows how to count them, in people, not just percents.

Sources: ASCEND trial, as priced in the essay. Number needed to treat: Laupacis A, Sackett DL, Roberts RS. New England Journal of Medicine. 1988.
