What If You Ran a Marathon on the Moon?

Below half of Earth's gravity, running turns cheaper than walking. What would 42.195 kilometers cost where the gaits trade places?

The headline number
The walk to run switch falls from 2.16 meters per second on Earth to 1.18 on the Moon, and running there costs about 2 joules per kilogram per meter

Gravity writes the rules of gait. On Earth, walking is the cheap gait and running the expensive one, and the body switches between them near 2.16 meters per second. Turn gravity down and the switch moves with it.

The essay turns a gravity dial from Earth to the Moon and watches the cost curves trade places, then counts Apollo's 38 classified gait events: 10 walks, 10 lopes, 18 runs. A labeled model halves a marathon clock under stated assumptions, and then the wall arrives: suit supplies, heat, and a excursion record of 7 hours 37 minutes.

The same Froude arithmetic that sets the switch on the Moon also governs body weight support in the rehab gym, which is where the essay lands.

Sources: Carr CE, McGee DJ. PLoS ONE. 2009. Pavei G, et al. Journal of Experimental Biology. 2015. Farley CT, McMahon TA. Journal of Biomechanics. 1992. NASA Apollo 17 mission records, Lunar and Planetary Institute.
