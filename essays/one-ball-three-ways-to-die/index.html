One Ball, Three Ways to Die

Why does the hip ball fail, and how long does its replacement last?

The headline number
Alpha angle: 54 degrees in the average painless hip; above 83 degrees tracked with osteoarthritis

The hip is a ball in a socket, and the ball fails three ways: born the wrong shape, starved of blood, or worn down by osteoarthritis, a whole-joint disease.

Shape is measured by the alpha angle. The average painless hip sits at 54 degrees, and in the CHECK cohort an alpha angle above 83 degrees tracked with developing osteoarthritis. That is an association from a cohort study, not a verdict on any single hip.

Then comes the replacement, and the essay reads it the way an actuary reads a life table: how long the new ball lasts, and what ends it.

Sources: Agricola R, et al. Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases. 2013. Frank JM, et al. American Journal of Sports Medicine. 2015.
