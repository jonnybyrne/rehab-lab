What If You Had Only One Lung?

Remove a whole lung and spirometry predicts half of everything. Why did the exercise test fall by only a fifth?

The headline number
Six months after pneumonectomy, lung volumes were down about a third, but maximal oxygen uptake was down only 20 percent (Bolliger et al. 1996)

In a Basel resection study, 68 patients were tested before surgery and again at three and six months: 18 had a whole lung removed, and 50 had mixed partial resections. Indexed to each group's own preoperative mean, the whole lung group's volumes fell about a third while its oxygen uptake fell only a fifth.

The essay runs a brass gauge board through its measured states, watches the most reported reason for stopping change from leg fatigue to breathlessness, then follows separate cohorts outward: a late cohort walking at 89 percent of predicted while peak oxygen uptake sat at 48 percent, one woman whose remaining lung appeared to grow over 15 years, and a follow up reaching back three decades.

These were surgical cohorts, mean age about 60, mostly cancer resections. The numbers describe what was measured in them, not what any one person's remaining lung will do.

Sources: Bolliger CT, et al. European Respiratory Journal. 1996. Vainshelboim B, et al. 2015. Butler JP, et al. New England Journal of Medicine. 2012. Laros CD, Westermann CJJ. 1987.
