What If One Week in Intensive Care Ate Your Muscles?

Fed, resting, and losing muscle by the day: what is actually doing the eating?

The headline number
Rectus femoris cross-sectional area fell 12.5 percent by day 7 and 17.7 percent by day 10 in 63 critically ill patients (Puthucheary et al., JAMA 2013)

In two hospitals in England, an ultrasound probe returned to the same spot on the same thigh on days 1, 3, 7, and 10. The patients were enrolled only if they were expected to be intubated for more than 48 hours, to spend more than 7 days in critical care, and to survive the stay. The muscle shrank fast: down 12.5 percent by day 7 and 17.7 percent by day 10.

The loss ran at two speeds. By day 7, patients with several failing organs had lost 15.7 percent against 3.0 percent with one failing organ. In the 28 patients assessed by all three mass measures, fibre area in the vastus lateralis fell 17.5 percent in a week, and by day 10 necrosis had appeared in 20 of 37 patients with serial biopsies.

The mechanism is a balance tipped the wrong way: protein synthesis was not shut down, but protein breakdown remained elevated at both measured time points, days 1 and 7. The study is observational, so it cannot prove failing organs cause the wasting, and an ultrasound measures the size of a muscle, never its strength. The essay prints those limits on the page.

Sources: Puthucheary ZA, et al. JAMA. 2013;310(15):1591 to 1600.
