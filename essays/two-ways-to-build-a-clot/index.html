Two Ways to Build a Clot

How do artery clots and vein clots form differently, and which drug blocks which?

The headline number
Platelets: 31.3 percent of coronary clot volume; 0.4 percent of vein clot volume

Blood builds a clot two different ways, in two different kinds of river. In a fast artery, the working structure is a plug of platelets; in a slow vein, it is a net of fibrin thrown over trapped red cells.

A laboratory that pulled real clots out of real people and took them apart measured the difference: platelets made up 31.3 percent of coronary artery clot volume, and 0.4 percent of vein clot volume.

The essay matches each build to the drug that blocks it, and shows why the strongest thinner in the cabinet can be the wrong drug for a clot it can reach.

Sources: Chernysh IN, et al. Scientific Reports. 2020.
