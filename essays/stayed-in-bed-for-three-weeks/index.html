What If You Stayed in Bed for Three Weeks?

Twenty days in bed cost five young men more fitness than thirty years of aging did. What happens when the two clocks race on one gauge?

The headline number
Maximal oxygen uptake fell from 3.3 to 2.4 liters per minute in 20 days of bed rest, a 27 percent loss; 30 years of aging cost about 12 percent, and six months of training reversed the 30 year decline

In 1966, five healthy 20 year old men in Dallas spent 20 days at complete bed rest. Their maximal oxygen uptake fell 27 percent, from 3.3 to 2.4 liters per minute (Saltin et al. 1968).

Thirty years later, the same five men had lost only about 12 percent to aging (McGuire et al. 2001), and six months of endurance training at about age 50 reversed 100 percent of that 30 year decline. At the 40 year reading, their baseline had fallen to the bed rest value itself, 2.4 liters per minute (Mitchell et al. 2019).

The essay races a 20 day bed clock against a 30 year life clock on one VO2max tank, follows the cascade from heart to muscle to bone, and lands on the modern ward, where older inpatients still spend most of the stay lying in bed. Five young healthy men are a demonstration, not a dosage.

Sources: Saltin B, et al. Circulation. 1968. McGuire DK, et al. Circulation. 2001. Mitchell JH, et al. 2019. Dirks ML, et al. 2016. LeBlanc AD, et al. 1990. Brown CJ, et al. 2009.
