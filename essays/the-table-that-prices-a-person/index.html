The Table That Prices a Person

How do life expectancy tables decide which care gets funded after spinal cord injury?

The headline number
Life expectancy at 20, C1 to C4 injury: 43.03 years; 33.74 with a pack a day

Ask how long a person will live after a spinal cord injury, and the answer comes from a table. That table was built to set prices, and it decides how much care gets funded.

In one prospective analysis the essay rebuilds, a 20-year-old man with a C1 to C4 injury and no risk behaviors had an estimated life expectancy of 43.03 years. At a pack of cigarettes a day or more, the estimate was 33.74 years: one habit, 9.29 years.

The essay opens the machinery up: how the years are counted, why the gains stalled, and why long-term survival has not changed over the past 30 years.

Sources: Shavelle RM, et al. Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation. 2015. Strauss DJ, et al. Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation. 2006.
