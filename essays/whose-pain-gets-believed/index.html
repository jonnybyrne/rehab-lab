Whose pain gets believed?

Whose pain gets believed, and who actually gets treated?

The headline number
Same fractures: analgesia 74 percent (white patients) versus 57 percent (Black patients)

Pain has no lab test; the only instrument is belief. The essay starts with the cleanest case in medicine: a broken bone, proven on imaging, with severe pain documented.

In that study, white patients received analgesia 74 percent of the time and Black patients 57 percent. Seven years earlier, Hispanic patients with the same fractures were twice as likely to receive no analgesia at all.

These are associations from observational studies, and the essay is careful about what could produce them. It asks who gets believed, and what that belief is worth in treatment.

Sources: Todd et al. Annals of Emergency Medicine. 2000. Todd et al. JAMA. 1993. Hoffman et al. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. 2016.
