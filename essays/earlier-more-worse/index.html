Earlier, More, Worse

The biggest stroke rehab trial ever run tested earlier, more frequent, higher-dose mobilisation. Why were patients less likely to be independent three months later?

The headline number
Independence at 3 months: 46 percent with very early mobilisation against 50 percent under usual care; adjusted odds ratio 0.73 (AVERT Trial Collaboration, The Lancet 2015)

AVERT randomised 2,104 patients across 56 acute stroke units in five countries. One group was assigned to very early mobilisation: frequent, higher dose sessions begun within 24 hours of stroke onset. The other received usual stroke unit care. The prescription was actually delivered: 92 percent of the early group were mobilised within 24 hours, against 59 percent under usual care.

The group moved earliest and most did worse. Fewer of them were independent at three months, deaths and serious adverse events gave no comfort, and the immobility complications the whole idea was meant to prevent showed no detected reduction.

A second, prespecified analysis pulled the dose apart: each extra session per day was associated with better odds, while each additional five minutes of out-of-bed activity per day pointed the other way. It is a secondary analysis, not a randomised comparison, and the essay keeps it labelled that way. AVERT randomised a bundle, so it cannot say which ingredient carried the harm; the best schedule for an individual patient remains uncertain.

Sources: AVERT Trial Collaboration group. The Lancet. 2015;386:46 to 55. Bernhardt J, et al. Neurology. 2016;86:2138 to 2145. Bernhardt J, Dewey HM, Thrift AG, Donnan GA. Stroke. 2004;35:1005 to 1009.
