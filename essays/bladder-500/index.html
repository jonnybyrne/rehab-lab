Why 500 mL for everyone?

One flat catheterization limit for every body on the ward: where did it come from, and should it scale?

The headline number
50.2 percent of 8,016 catheterization measurements were guideline-discordant under a definition of volume over 500 mL and/or interval over 6 hours (He et al., Spinal Cord 2025)

Adult bladder guidelines write one flat number: keep catheterized volumes under 500 mL, catheterize four to six times a day. The rule is the same for every body on the ward, and no adult guideline found stratifies it by weight, height, or body mass index.

Pediatrics writes the rule differently: expected bladder capacity in infants runs at about 7 mL per kilogram, and schoolchild functional capacity tracks body size. In adults, the size evidence is weak: body size did not predict holding capacity in the studies that looked, while higher body mass raised pressures at capacity rather than capacity itself.

What clinicians actually individualise on is the measured bladder: its own capacity and compliance, read from diaries, scanners, and urodynamics. Nobody has tested size-adjusted cath thresholds in adult spinal cord injury, and the wearable monitors that might one day personalise the number are not yet validated in adults with spinal cord injury.

Sources: Alberta Health Services. Spinal Cord Injury Bladder Management Pathway. He et al. Spinal Cord. 2025. Fairhurst JJ, et al. J Pediatr Surg. 1991. Richter HE, et al. (PRIDE). 2008.
