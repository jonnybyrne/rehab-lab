The Patient Who Was In There

If a patient cannot move or speak, how do you decide whether anyone is home?

The headline number
18 of 44 (41 percent) reclassified as minimally conscious on the CRS-R

After a severe brain injury, some patients open their eyes but give no sign that anyone is home. The bedside team has to decide what that silence means.

In a 2009 study of 103 patients, every patient was classified twice: once by the consensus of the treating team, and once with a standardized scale, the CRS-R. Scaled to the essay’s ward, 18 of 44 patients labeled vegetative by consensus met criteria for the minimally conscious state on the scale: 41 percent.

The essay lets you examine three patients and commit to a call before it shows you the whole ward, and it is explicit about the study’s limits, including raters who were not masked.

Sources: Schnakers C, et al. BMC Neurology. 2009. Giacino JT, et al. Neurology. 2018.
