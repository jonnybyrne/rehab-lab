The typical spinal cord injury patient is 15 years older than in 1973.

Who gets a spinal cord injury today, and how long do they live?

The headline number
Mean age at injury: 28.7 years (1972 to 1979) to 43.2 years (newest cohort series)

The mean age at spinal cord injury rose from 28.7 years in the 1972 to 1979 cohort to 43.2 in the most recent cohort series, and a newer data window puts the current anchor at 44.3.

Causes tilted with age: vehicular crashes accounted for 46.9 percent of injuries in the 1970s cohort, and falls have climbed as patients have grown older.

The essay reads half a century of registry data and asks whether the rehab system built around the young trauma patient has caught up.

Sources: National Spinal Cord Injury Statistical Center (NSCISC). 2026 Data Sheet.
