What happens to your body at every hour of a marathon?

How does a marathon change your body, hour by hour?

The headline number
13 percent of 488 Boston finishers had hyponatremia (2005)

One marathon, told hour by hour: three runners, one race clock, and every physiological beat anchored to a published number.

Among 488 Boston Marathon finishers studied in 2005, 13 percent had exercise-associated hyponatremia, and gaining weight during the race was the strongest signal.

Muscle damage runs on a slower clock: mean creatine kinase reached 3,424 U/L a day after the 1979 Boston Marathon.

Sources: Almond et al. New England Journal of Medicine. 2005. Siegel et al. Yale Journal of Biology and Medicine. 1980.
