The boom
Serve

Pickleball grew from 3.46 million players to 19.81 million.

In 2019, pickleball was already shaking off the retirement-community stereotype. Five years later, the SFIA survey counted 19.81 million U.S. players, up 45.8% in a single year and the fastest-growing sport four years running.

And yes, that denominator is loose: SFIA counts anyone age six or older who played even once that year. These are survey estimates, not registrations or regular players.

The headline is true.

Nationally estimated ED injuries rose from 8,894 in 2020 to 16,997 in 2022, a 91% jump. The series dipped 14% in 2020, then climbed 41% into 2021. That dip lines up with pandemic closures and disrupted care. A pandemic closure artifact is the plausible reading, not an established one, and it is not proof the sport briefly got safer.

Participation rose faster.

Over the same endpoint years, SFIA’s player estimate more than doubled: 4.20 million to 8.95 million. The injury curve is real, and so is the growth curve underneath it.

Divide by players.

Divide injuries by players and the explosion flattens to about two ED visits per 1,000 players per year: 2.1 in 2020, 1.9 in 2022. The numerator is NEISS's ED estimate and the denominator is SFIA's survey count. They were never collected as a pair.

Flat is not precise.

NEISS misses urgent care, office, PT, and self-managed injuries, while SFIA counts one-time players. The early injury estimates carry wide uncertainty. “Roughly flat” is the strongest claim these mismatched denominators can support.

The line the data supportsPickleball did not clearly become more dangerous per player. There were simply far more players available to get hurt.
Read the rate with its uncertainty attached
  • Two unlike counts. The rate divides NEISS's ED injury estimates by SFIA's player survey. Neither was collected to be the other's denominator, and the survey counts a person who played once the same as a person who plays daily.
  • The player base is not the ED sample. The age and sex splits in this essay come from ED case series (Cheng, 2020 to 2022; Yu, 2013 to 2022; Weiss, 2010 to 2019, seniors only). Those samples are not the player survey, and this essay's sources do not supply a player-base sex split to divide the ED shares by. A share of ED fractures is not a share of players.
  • Small numbers move ratios a lot. NEISS estimates for a young sport rest on few sampled cases, so the early estimates carry wide uncertainty. The 9.3× wrist-fracture odds below, for example, carries a 95% confidence interval from 3.6 to 23.9.
  • Different windows, different definitions. Each series counts different years, ages, and case types. Yu excluded an estimated 11,629 cardiac, heat, and other non-musculoskeletal cases from its mechanism analysis; the other series did not draw the line in the same place.

Match point

The fracture curve is a participation curve.

An active older population doesn’t need to go back to the couch. It needs safer entry into the sport: equipment that matches the court, preparation that matches the movement, and a health plan that matches the player.

One more case-definition trapAmong senior pickleball cases in Weiss et al., 11.1% were cardiovascular non-injuries. NEISS records medical events linked to the activity; not every case is a musculoskeletal injury.

So is pickleball dangerous? Sort of. Mostly it’s crowded.

Methodology & sources

What this curve can and cannot say

  • ED visits only: NEISS does not see urgent care, office, physical therapy, or self-managed injuries.
  • Weighted estimates: national numbers come from a probability sample; early pickleball years have wide confidence intervals.
  • Different studies, different case definitions: Yu excluded 11,629 nationally estimated cardiac, heat, and non-musculoskeletal cases from its mechanism analysis; Cheng and Weiss used different windows and classifications.
  • Survey denominator: SFIA participation is based on about 20,000 online interviews, ages 6+, and counts at least one play per year.
  • Derived rate: the 2.1 and 1.9 per 1,000 figures divide Cheng’s NEISS endpoints by SFIA participation. They are our calculations, not published incidence rates.
  • The 2020 dip: the 14% fall in 2020 reflects disrupted access and play, not a modeled safety improvement.
  • Player-base contrast: SFIA reported ages 25 to 34 as the largest 2023 cohort at 2.3M; a full comparable age distribution is not supplied, so only the reported cohort is shown.
  • Chart data: the participation series, ED endpoints, and age anchors used here are in data/pickleball-fracture-curve/.
